# Demo script and submission draft: language access (issue #87, for #30)

Draft for the team to rehearse and edit. Everything here matches what the app does today. Do not add numbers, quotes, or outcomes that are not in this file: there are none.

## One-line pitch

Feeling sick is hard. Explaining it in another language should not be. Sickway is a synthetic prototype that helps a student who is less comfortable in English say how they feel, check what the clinic will see, and get instructions back in their language.

## Three-minute demo (two devices, one session)

**Setup (before the timer).** Laptop on the landing page. Press **Start demo session**, show the QR code from the **Demo** menu, scan it with the phone. Set the phone to **Español**.

1. **The barrier (15 s).** Read the headline. Say: “Our fictional student knows exactly how they feel. The hard part is saying it in English, and knowing the clinic understood.”
2. **Guided expression (40 s, phone).** Open “¿No sabe cómo decirlo?”. Point out the three questions and that they only guide: nothing is filled in. Type: “Desde ayer por la mañana tengo fiebre de 102 y me duele todo el cuerpo. Tengo un examen hoy a las 2 y no sé si debo ir.”
3. **Student control (40 s, phone).** Answer the checklist. On review, show **Lo que usted escribió** above the details, the onset marked unconfirmed until the student confirms it, and “no informado” staying “no informado”. Tick the separate consent. Submit.
4. **The handoff, and its limit (50 s, laptop).** Open the intake. Read the English brief: it quotes the student's Spanish phrases. Show **In the student's own words** beside it and read the notice aloud: nothing is translated, and the brief does not show that a language gap has been resolved. Say: “We disclose this limit. We do not solve it. Translation and interpreters are out of scope.”
5. **Options and packet (25 s, laptop).** Show the mock options (every value labelled mock), pick one, note both instruction languages are preselected from the student's preference, **Confirm and attach**.
6. **Back to the student (10 s, phone).** The ticket appears. Open it: prewritten instructions in Spanish.

**Fallback.** If extraction is slow or fails, the screen keeps the typed text and offers “Enter details myself”. The English flow with the prepared case is the second fallback. Never present the prepared case as something the student typed; the app labels it on every screen.

## Submission copy (draft)

**Inspiration.** Being sick is stressful. Being sick and having to explain it in a second language is harder: finding symptom words, following healthcare terms, and not knowing whether you were understood. That is a communication gap, not a gap in intelligence or in knowing your own body.

**What it does.** A synthetic, two-device workflow: student intake, a clinician brief, mock access options, and a returned packet. The screens are in English and Spanish. Optional plain-language prompts help a student say what feels wrong, when it started, and what they want understood. The student reviews and corrects every detail against their own words and gives separate consent. The clinician sees a traceable brief with the student's original wording beside it, and sends back prewritten instructions in the languages the student chose.

**What is honest about it.** Everything is synthetic: the patient, plan, prices, stock, and resources are labelled mock where they appear. Nothing is booked, prescribed, verified, or sent anywhere. What a student types is never translated; an English brief can contain Spanish phrases, and the clinician screen says so. The Spanish copy was written with an AI assistant and read by a Spanish-speaking teammate in part; it is not professionally or clinically reviewed.

**Intended impact.** Fewer students staying silent because of language. We have not measured this. There are no real users, no research, and no outcome data.

**What is next.** Professional translation review, more languages, and a separate, careful decision about interpreter or translation support at the handoff, with real students and clinicians involved.
